# TENS Therapy

## What TENS is
TENS (Transcutaneous Electrical Nerve Stimulation) sends small, safe electrical pulses through pads (electrodes) placed on the skin. It reduces pain in two ways: by "closing the pain gate" (gate control - blocking pain signals in the spinal cord) and by helping the body release its own painkillers (endorphins / endogenous opioids). TENS relieves pain but does not heal the cause. The SmartHeal wearable has built-in TENS pads.

## The six TENS parameters
- **Frequency (Hz)** - how many pulses per second. It selects the pain-relief mechanism (gate control vs opioid release).
- **Pulse width (µs)** - how long each pulse lasts. It controls how deep the current reaches and which nerve fibres are recruited.
- **Intensity (mA)** - how strong the current is. It must be in the therapeutic window between too little and too much.
- **Mode** - how pulses are delivered (continuous, burst or modulated), which affects comfort over a session.
- **Session duration (minutes)** - how long the session runs.
- **Electrode placement** - where the pads go, which decides whether the current reaches the right nerve pathway.

## TENS frequency
- **80-150 Hz - Conventional TENS**: activates A-beta fibres -> gate control. Fast onset (minutes). Best for acute pain and activity-related pain.
- **2-10 Hz - Acupuncture-like TENS (AL-TENS)**: activates A-delta fibres plus muscle contractions -> endogenous opioid release. Slow onset (20-30 minutes). Best for chronic, persistent pain, with longer-lasting relief.

## TENS pulse width
- **50-100 µs** - more selective, superficial A-beta fibres. Comfortable skin sensation, less discomfort.
- **150-300 µs** - broader, deeper A-delta and motor fibres. Motor response and the opioid pain-relief pathway.

## TENS intensity
- **Conventional TENS: Strong But Comfortable (SBC)** - clearly felt and strong, but never painful.
- **AL-TENS: motor threshold** - visible muscle twitching under the pads.
- **Burst TENS: motor threshold (rhythmic)** - rhythmic, pulsing muscle twitches.

## TENS modes compared
- **Conventional TENS**: 80-150 Hz, 50-200 µs, strong but comfortable, gate control, fast onset. Used for quick, temporary relief of acute pain (sudden injuries, post-surgery soreness) during everyday activities.
- **AL-TENS**: 2-10 Hz, 150-300 µs, motor threshold, opioid release, slow onset (20-30 min). Used for long-lasting relief of chronic, deep muscle pain when the patient can tolerate strong twitching.
- **Burst TENS**: 100 Hz internal bursts delivered 2-4 times per second, 150-250 µs, motor threshold, gate control plus opioid release, moderate onset. A more comfortable, rhythmic alternative for chronic pain when AL-TENS feels too intense.

## TENS session duration
- **20-60 minutes** is the typical clinical range. Adjust to tolerance and response.

## TENS electrode placement
- **Local pain**: pads over or immediately around the painful area.
- **Referred or neuropathic pain**: pads along the dermatome, following the involved nerve's path.
- Keep pads at least about 2.5 cm (1 inch) apart. Place them on clean, dry, unbroken skin.

## When NOT to use TENS (contraindications)
- **Pacemaker, implanted defibrillator or any implanted electronic device** - do not use.
- **Heart conditions** - never place pads across the chest or over the heart.
- **Pregnancy** - not over the abdomen, pelvis or lower back (except under medical supervision in labour).
- **Epilepsy** - not on the head or neck.
- Never on the front or sides of the neck (carotid area), on the head, over the eyes, or inside the mouth.
- Not over broken skin, rashes, numb areas, cancerous areas or areas with a blood clot (DVT).
- Do not use while driving, operating machinery, sleeping or in the bath/shower.
- Undiagnosed pain should be checked by a doctor first.

## TENS safe use tips
- Turn the device off before placing or removing pads.
- Start at the lowest intensity and increase slowly until it feels strong but comfortable.
- A tingling or buzzing feeling is normal. Stop if you feel burning, sharp pain or skin irritation.
- If the sensation fades during a session (your nerves get used to it), increase intensity slightly or switch to a modulated or burst mode.
