# Heat Therapy

## What heat therapy is
Heat therapy (thermotherapy) warms the skin and the tissue underneath. Warmth widens blood vessels, increases blood flow, relaxes tight muscles, reduces stiffness and makes tissues more flexible. The SmartHeal wearable has a built-in heater with three settings: Low, Medium and High.

## Heat settings (SmartHeal guidelines)
- **Low: 37°C - 40°C** - best for long sessions, deep joint stiffness and chronic aches. Maximum session: **30-40 minutes**.
- **Medium: 40°C - 46°C** - best for general muscle soreness and mild spasms. Maximum session: **20-25 minutes**.
- **High: above 46°C** - use with extreme care only. Maximum session: **15 minutes**.
- The High setting risks skin burns if used beyond 15 minutes.
- Always monitor the skin during and after heat therapy.

## When heat helps
- Chronic (long-term, more than about 6 weeks) muscle or joint pain and stiffness.
- Muscle tightness, knots and mild spasms.
- Morning stiffness, for example in osteoarthritis.
- Warming up muscles before stretching or light exercise.
- Old injuries that are no longer swollen or warm.

## When NOT to use heat (contraindications)
- Fresh injuries (roughly the first 48-72 hours) - sprains, strains or bruises that are still swelling. Use cold instead.
- Swelling that is increasing, or a joint that is red, hot and inflamed.
- Open wounds, burns, broken or infected skin.
- Areas with reduced feeling (numbness, diabetic neuropathy) - you may not notice a burn.
- Poor circulation, deep vein thrombosis (DVT) or bleeding disorders.
- Over a pregnant abdomen, over known tumours, or over skin treated with heat rubs or creams.
- Do not sleep with heat on, and never use it on someone who cannot report discomfort.

## Heat vs cold - which one?
- **Cold (ice)** for new injuries and swelling in the first 48-72 hours: it reduces swelling and numbs pain. 10-15 minutes wrapped in a cloth.
- **Heat** for stiffness, tight muscles and long-term aches once swelling has gone.
- If unsure whether an injury is still inflamed (warm, swollen, red), choose cold or ask a professional.

## Safe use tips
- Start on Low and only go higher if comfortable.
- The skin may turn slightly pink - that is normal. Stop if it becomes bright red, blistered, very painful or numb.
- Keep a layer of clothing between the device and skin if it feels too hot.
- Leave at least 1-2 hours between heat sessions on the same area.
