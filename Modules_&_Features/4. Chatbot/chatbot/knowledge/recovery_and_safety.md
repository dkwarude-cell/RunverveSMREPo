# Recovery and Safety

## Acute vs chronic pain
- **Acute pain** is new and usually linked to an injury (a sprain, strain, bruise) - typically under 6 weeks. Swelling, warmth and redness mean the area is still inflamed.
- **Chronic pain** lasts longer than about 3 months, for example long-term back pain, neck pain or arthritis.
- Acute pain usually suits cold, rest and conventional TENS. Chronic pain usually suits heat, mid-frequency vibration, and AL-TENS or burst TENS.

## First aid for a new soft-tissue injury (PEACE and LOVE)
First days - **PEACE**:
- **Protect** - avoid movements that increase pain for 1-3 days.
- **Elevate** - raise the area above heart level when possible.
- **Avoid anti-inflammatories** in the first days unless a doctor advises (they may slow healing), and avoid heat.
- **Compress** - an elastic bandage helps limit swelling.
- **Educate** - recovery takes time; avoid unnecessary treatments.
After the first days - **LOVE**:
- **Load** - gradually return to normal activity as pain allows.
- **Optimism** - a positive mindset helps recovery.
- **Vascularisation** - pain-free cardio (walking, cycling) boosts blood flow.
- **Exercise** - restore strength, mobility and balance.
Cold packs (10-15 minutes, wrapped in a cloth) can ease pain in the first 48-72 hours.

## Common conditions and therapy ideas
- **Muscle soreness after exercise (DOMS)**: medium heat, mid-frequency vibration (20-50 Hz), gentle stretching, hydration.
- **Lower back pain (non-specific)**: heat (low to medium), staying gently active, conventional TENS for flare-ups or AL-TENS / burst for long-term pain.
- **Neck and shoulder stiffness**: low or medium heat and gentle mobility exercises. Never place TENS pads on the front or sides of the neck.
- **Knee osteoarthritis / joint stiffness**: low heat for longer sessions, TENS for pain, strengthening exercises.
- **Ankle or wrist sprain (new)**: PEACE first, cold not heat, no vibration over the injury in the first days. Conventional TENS may help pain.
- **Tendon pain (tendinopathy)**: relative rest, gradual loading exercises, heat for stiffness once not acutely inflamed.

## Red flags - see a doctor urgently
Seek medical help immediately (call 112 in India, or your local emergency number) if there is:
- Chest pain, pressure or pain spreading to the arm or jaw, or trouble breathing.
- Signs of stroke: face drooping, arm weakness, slurred speech.
- Severe bleeding, a deep wound, or a bone that looks deformed or may be broken.
- Unable to bear weight or move a joint after an injury.
- Numbness, tingling or weakness that is spreading, or loss of bladder or bowel control with back pain.
- A hot, red, swollen joint with fever, or a wound with spreading redness, pus or fever (possible infection).
- A cold, pale or blue limb, or a swollen, painful calf (possible blood clot).
- Severe head injury, confusion or fainting.

## See a doctor or physiotherapist soon if
- Pain is not improving after 1-2 weeks of self-care, or keeps getting worse.
- Pain wakes you at night or is present even at rest.
- You have diabetes, a heart condition, are pregnant, or have an implanted device and want to use therapy devices.
- You are unsure what the injury is.

## Healthy habits for recovery
- Sleep 7-9 hours - most tissue repair happens during sleep.
- Stay hydrated and eat enough protein, fruit and vegetables.
- Keep gently active - complete rest usually slows recovery from muscle and joint pain.
- Warm up before exercise and increase training load gradually (about 10% per week).
- Good posture and regular breaks from sitting help neck and back pain.
