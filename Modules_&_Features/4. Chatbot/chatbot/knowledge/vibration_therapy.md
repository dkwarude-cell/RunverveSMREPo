# Vibration Therapy

## What vibration therapy is
Vibration therapy uses small motors that oscillate against the body. Depending on the settings, it can stimulate nerves, improve circulation, relax or activate muscles and reduce pain. The SmartHeal wearable has vibration motors that are controlled by four parameters: frequency, amplitude, duty cycle and session timer.

## The four vibration parameters
- **Frequency (Hz)** - how fast the motor oscillates per second. It decides which nerve fibres and clinical effects are triggered.
- **Amplitude (mm)** - how far the motor physically moves back and forth. It decides whether the vibration targets nerves or deep tissue.
- **Duty cycle** - the on/off pulsing pattern, which stops the nervous system getting used to the vibration.
- **Session timer (minutes)** - how long the vibration runs continuously.

## Vibration frequency settings
- **Low: 5-20 Hz** - balance, proprioception (body-position sense) and basic blood circulation.
- **Mid: 20-50 Hz** - muscle power, pain relief and reducing spasticity (muscle stiffness).
- **High: 100-150 Hz** - Tonic Vibration Reflex (TVR): automatic reflex muscle contractions used in motor rehabilitation.

## Vibration amplitude settings
- **Low: 0.2-0.5 mm** - targets neurological pathways (Ia afferent nerve fibres).
- **High: 1.0-2.0 mm** - deep-tissue mobilisation. Cap it carefully to avoid bruising.

## Duty cycle and patterns
- **Stochastic Resonance** - a variable, noisy, randomised pulse. Used for "waking up" damaged nerves, for example in diabetic neuropathy.
- **Intermittent duty cycle** - 5 seconds on, 2 seconds rest. Prevents the nervous system from getting used to (habituating to) the vibration.

## Vibration session time
- **0-30 minutes** is the standard clinical threshold.
- Over-exposure risks vascular fatigue or nerve desensitisation, so do not exceed 30 minutes in one session.

## When vibration helps
- Muscle soreness after exercise (DOMS) and muscle tightness.
- Pain relief for muscle and joint aches (mid frequency).
- Improving circulation and balance (low frequency).
- Motor rehabilitation after nerve or muscle weakness (high frequency, under guidance).

## When NOT to use vibration (contraindications)
- Fresh injuries, fractures or suspected fractures, and acute inflammation.
- Over recent surgery sites, open wounds or skin infections.
- Deep vein thrombosis (DVT) or blood clots, and bleeding disorders or blood thinners (higher bruising risk).
- Pregnancy (avoid the abdomen and lower back).
- Over implanted devices such as pacemakers, joint replacements or metal implants unless a doctor approves.
- Severe osteoporosis, and directly over the spine, eyes or head.

## Safe use tips
- Start at low frequency and low amplitude, then increase slowly.
- Stop if you feel sharp pain, numbness, tingling that does not go away, or see bruising.
- Use the intermittent pattern for longer sessions.
